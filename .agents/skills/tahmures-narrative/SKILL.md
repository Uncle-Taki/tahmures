# Tahmures narrative canon

Use with `$mehdi-narrative` for Tahmures stories, character or place changes,
mythic claims, narrative localization, and continuity audits. The generic
skill owns story craft and analogy boundaries; this skill owns the project's
mythic evidence tiers and cast constraints. It does not authorize manuscript
rewrites. Current user instructions, `AGENTS.md`, `book/project-charter.md`,
`book/narrative-continuity.md`, and `meta-prompt.md` retain their authority;
the dossier is research input, not a replacement for those contracts.

## Read only what the task needs

- `references/canon.md`: direct *Shahnameh* roster, relationships, motifs, and
  source cautions. Read for character choice or a claim about Ferdowsi.
- `references/later-traditions.md`: later material and unverified dossier
  claims. Read for Avestan/Rivayat elements, Merv, Shiraz, or death stories.
- `references/story-world.md`: established fictional cast, chronology, naming,
  and Tahmures's cumulative arc. Read for story creation or localization.
- `references/character-migration.md`: the semantic replacement procedure.
  Read before renaming or replacing any existing role.

## Evidence and fiction boundary

Label each proposed element before use: `TIER_A_SHAHNAMEH_EXPLICIT`,
`TIER_B_LATER_TRADITION`, or `TIER_C_PROJECT_FICTION`. Record the source or
continuity entry and the intended narrative function. Tier B or C never
becomes Tier A through repetition. A sourced mythic motif is a palette, not a
mandatory decoration. The chapter episodes remain original teaching fiction
(`narrative_origin: original-teaching-fiction`), never reconstructed Ferdowsi.

For Tier A assertions use the cited primary verses or a scholarly edition;
do not trust dossier paraphrase where the verse is narrower. Attribute Tier B
as later tradition only when a reliable source supports it. Treat unverified
place-founder and genealogy variants as leads, not book facts. Do not invent a
quotation or silently merge incompatible traditions.

## Story decision procedure

1. Read the affected tale, technical bridge, frozen English/Persian counterpart
   when applicable, and the continuity entry. Identify its actual failure
   owner, causal teaching point, existing character functions, and hook.
2. Classify every named character, role, place, and mythic motif by tier and
   chronology. Check Hushang as predecessor/father and Jamshid as son/successor
   in the chosen *Shahnameh* framing; a different chronology needs an explicit
   project decision. Do not treat all three kings as routine contemporaneous
   court staff.
3. Map character **function before name** using
   `references/character-migration.md`. Shahrasp may counsel and challenge
   governance; he is not the default scribe, guard, runtime, or soldier. Keep
   an unnamed specialist when its work matters more than a proper name. Divs
   may be capable, deceptive, uncertain, or useful; not every failure is theirs.
4. Preserve the accepted ancient-world register. Keep technical vocabulary in
   the tale-to-engineering bridge, and keep metaphor similarity, difference,
   and boundary explicit. Never alter a validated engineering claim to fit a
   story or imply an unimplemented capability.
5. Preserve accumulated character development across Parts I–IV. Check named
   cast economy, recurring objects, location identity, and setup/payoff. If a
   later approved manuscript edit changes any of these, update
   `book/narrative-continuity.md` in that same change, English first and Persian
   synchronization afterward.

The current English source is frozen. An audit or migration proposal does not
authorize a name change in either edition. Obtain a scoped narrative revision
decision before changing source text; account for translation and human-review
consequences afterward.

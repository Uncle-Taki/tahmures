# Project story world and continuity

`book/narrative-continuity.md` is the editable authority for the existing
fictional cast, objects, places, world rules, and chapter hooks. `meta-prompt.md`
sets the arc and ancient-world register; `book/project-charter.md` sets the
original-fiction/cultural-integrity boundary. This reference is a compact
reading guide, not a competing continuity ledger.

## Cast functions

Tahmures is continuous protagonist: Part I learns roles, evidence, and
requirements; Part II tests examples, schemas, workflows, variation, and
context; Part III governs retrieval, memory, tools, agents, and hand-offs;
Part IV evaluates, controls authority, observes operations, and teaches
mechanism choice. Each chapter may reveal a new blind spot without resetting
his earlier competence.

The First Scribe owns experimental records; Keeper of Scripts owns form and
representation; Archivist owns provenance and memory; Gatekeeper owns action
boundaries; Messenger owns transfer. Mirror, Literal, and Wandering Divs and the
Many-Voiced Council embody different capabilities or failure tendencies, not
fixed moral identities. Shahrasp is available as a counsellor, but no current
chapter tale uses him; add him only when counsel has a distinct dramatic job.

Use a small named cast. An unnamed court scribe, archivist, gatekeeper,
surveyor, mason, courier, farmer, judge, clerk, soldier, or craftsperson is
often more faithful to the causal lesson than a newly invented named hero.
Do not collapse these occupational roles into Shahrasp. Check pronouns and
agency against the actual tale as well as the continuity ledger.

## Places and naming

Existing recurring project-fiction places include the Hall of Scripts
(Persian `تالار دبیره‌ها`), Royal Archive, Council Chamber, Border and Northern
Gates, workshop of mechanical scribes, western pass, Chamber of Answers, Map
Room, and provincial relay towers. Keep their identities and hooks stable.
Varena and the province names Ray/Gorgan occur in live examples; their use is
not evidence that they are named in Ferdowsi's Tahmures episode. Merv and
Shiraz are not current tale locations. For new places, prefer a meaningful
Tahmures-world function and consistent English/Persian names; do not add an
ancient-sounding label merely for atmosphere.

Before renaming a hall, person, or object, search the continuity file, all
English tales, Persian counterparts, navigation, exercises, and code examples
for dependent references. Localize the function and setting; never use a blind
phrase substitution. A later approved English rename creates Persian
synchronization and potentially renewed human-review work.

Persian spelling of the protagonist's name: «طهمورث» is the human-approved
form (preference recorded 2026-10-02; scoped authorization 2026-10-10).
Persian Chapters 01–04 use «طهمورث» in prose and tale headings («روایت
طهمورث»), and the translation validator enforces that heading per chapter
range. Chapters 05–24 and `book/fa/translation-glossary.md` keep «تهمورث»
pending a separate book-wide terminology decision; the scoped alignment is
not a glossary promotion. New narrative work uses «طهمورث». Any later
extension to Chapters 05–24 needs its own scoped, both-edition revision
with continuity review.

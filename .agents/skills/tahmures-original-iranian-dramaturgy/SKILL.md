# Original Iranian dramatic craft — Beyzai-informed, never imitative

This is a **craft lens**, not a request to reproduce Bahram Beyzai's voice, phrasing, structures, or scenes. Draw on broadly describable properties identified in scholarship: Iranian theatrical forms, oral storytelling, masks and witnessed versions, myth examined from the margins, ambiguity of power and testimony, agency under hierarchy, symbolic performance, and performable heightened language. Sources: `story-room/references/CRAFT_AND_RESEARCH.md`.

## Non-negotiable source and approval rules
- Work inside the actual Tahmures repo. Read root/nested `AGENTS.md`, `meta-prompt.md`, `book/project-charter.md`, `book/narrative-continuity.md`, and the canonical English chapter relevant to the task. Follow project-local editorial skill and approvals. The English narrative and continuity are canonical for story facts; media adaptations have their own scoped approval.
- For Chapter 01, inspect `editorial/media-handoff/chapter-01-animation-status.json` and the selected revision under `Characters/animations/chapter-01-candidate-01/production/english-final-03/`, including its README, timeline and reviews, and check for documented superseding decisions.
- Do not assume an 11-scene count or change approved duration: verify the real timeline and flag any mismatch. Div-e Sefid serves as messenger in the approved Chapter 01 animation, while Chapar is a distinct courier. Do not merge them.
- Separate every claim or suggestion with labels **CANON**, **APPROVED MEDIA ADAPTATION**, **PROPOSAL**, **CONFLICT**, or **UNKNOWN**. Cite repo paths (and line/section/scene identifiers when available) for source-derived claims. A proposed line or scene is never an approved line or scene.
- Do not modify canonical English/Persian manuscripts, continuity, approved media, approvals, or status records without the owner's explicit permission. Do not create new voice, imagery, provider calls, uploads, commits, pushes, or deployments. Candidate writing stays in a new working folder.
- The episode must remain original teaching fiction distinct from *Shahnameh*: no modern engineering jargon in the ancient-world scene, a concrete chapter-specific failure with consequences, divs as capable and uncertain rather than just evil, a correctly identified failure owner, an explicit engineering bridge, and a hook to the next chapter.

## Procedure
1. Identify which existing Tahmures scene genuinely benefits from **one** of these devices: (a) competing witnesses retell one event differently; (b) a witnessed ritual makes a power relation visible; (c) an everyday object changes symbolic meaning through action; (d) a public proclamation contrasts with a private act; (e) a supposedly monstrous figure reasons more carefully than the court; (f) the outcome is known but the moral explanation changes.
2. Set the double reading: **literal plot action** + **ethical/social subtext**. Both must work independently. The metaphor must have a physical anchor and consequence, not only decorative language.
3. Where history/legend is involved, **interrogate** power, testimony, identity, and belonging rather than treating old stories as authority. Keep the fictional Tahmures plot distinct from *Shahnameh* and cite the canonical world rules.
4. Heighten selective lines through rhythm, parallel contrast, or repetition **only when a character needs the words as an action** (to accuse, deflect, stall, swear, threaten, mourn). In contrast, use plain speech and silence to sharpen the important line.
5. Keep characters' diction distinguishable. A messenger, ruler, guard, witness, and div must not all become grand orators. Persian-register proposals require language-native review and must not silently replace canonical English/Persian chapters.
6. Write two originals: **restrained** (subtext carried by action) and **ceremonial** (public ritual adds pressure). Compare performability, originality, comprehensibility, runtime, and budget. Choose only if clearly stronger.

## Explicit avoidances
- Never lift/paraphrase famous Beyzai lines, named scenes, plot reversals, verbal tics, or recognizable monologues.
- Do not claim a historical Iranian performance convention is mandatory in every episode.
- Avoid pseudo-archaic word salads, generic proverbs, moral sermonizing, exoticized mythology, and treating women or divs only as symbols.
- No modern AI/engineering vocabulary in ancient story dialogue.

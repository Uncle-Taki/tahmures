# Character pressure and adversarial forces

## Non-negotiable source and approval rules
- Work inside the actual Tahmures repo. Read root/nested `AGENTS.md`, `meta-prompt.md`, `book/project-charter.md`, `book/narrative-continuity.md`, and the canonical English chapter relevant to the task. Follow project-local editorial skill and approvals. The English narrative and continuity are canonical for story facts; media adaptations have their own scoped approval.
- For Chapter 01, inspect `editorial/media-handoff/chapter-01-animation-status.json` and the selected revision under `Characters/animations/chapter-01-candidate-01/production/english-final-03/`, including its README, timeline and reviews, and check for documented superseding decisions.
- Do not assume an 11-scene count or change approved duration: verify the real timeline and flag any mismatch. Div-e Sefid serves as messenger in the approved Chapter 01 animation, while Chapar is a distinct courier. Do not merge them.
- Separate every claim or suggestion with labels **CANON**, **APPROVED MEDIA ADAPTATION**, **PROPOSAL**, **CONFLICT**, or **UNKNOWN**. Cite repo paths (and line/section/scene identifiers when available) for source-derived claims. A proposed line or scene is never an approved line or scene.
- Do not modify canonical English/Persian manuscripts, continuity, approved media, approvals, or status records without the owner's explicit permission. Do not create new voice, imagery, provider calls, uploads, commits, pushes, or deployments. Candidate writing stays in a new working folder.
- The episode must remain original teaching fiction distinct from *Shahnameh*: no modern engineering jargon in the ancient-world scene, a concrete chapter-specific failure with consequences, divs as capable and uncertain rather than just evil, a correctly identified failure owner, an explicit engineering bridge, and a hook to the next chapter.

## Procedure
1. Inventory each character appearing in selected sources. For each: status, canonical description, knowledge at entrance, public objective, hidden need (inference), fear, value they protect, lie or blind spot, moral boundary, power/resources and limitation, and relation to the episode objective.
2. Map opposed **specific actions**: persuade, conceal, test, delay, bargain, protect, accuse, witness, sacrifice. Even allies may disagree on the right procedure; no cardboard villain. Account for what divs can do and how they can be mistaken.
3. Distinguish visible behavior from nonfilmable psychology. Express an inner pressure through an object, refusal, timing of an answer, or choice with consequences.
4. Track knowledge imbalance (`viewer knows`, `character A knows`, `character B falsely believes`) at each revelation; avoid characters knowing unearned facts.
5. Test female, marginal, non-royal, witness, and outsider figures for consequential agency where supported by the story; never insert a character merely to satisfy a checklist. Avoid automatically privileging the ruler's account.
6. Give each recurring character a voice fingerprint: sentence length, preferred metaphors rooted in their world, formality, manner of deflection, characteristic silence, and taboo subject. Avoid mimicking identifiable authored dialogue.
7. Test `remove this character`: what unique decision, information, resistance or cost disappears? Combine roles only when source and approvals permit; **never** combine Chapar and Div-e Sefid.

## Output
`02_CHARACTER_PRESSURE.md`: evidence-tagged conflict grid, desire vs obligation, knowledge map, agentic decisions, individual speech constraints, and unresolved approval items.

## Gate
Every central character changes another character's options. Antagonism may be institutional, procedural, or internal, but must become a visible obstacle.

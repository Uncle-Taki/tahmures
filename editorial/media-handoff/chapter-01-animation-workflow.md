## Phase 1 — storyboard and performance specification

**Codex deliverables:** an 11-scene shot board with subshots, camera/crop per shot,
background IDs, actor positions, prop custody, caption region, actual actions and
required assets. Give every action its failure owner and narrative purpose.

joint action, or separately drawn contact insert. Do not rig a body part unless

from actual candidates; recording approach. Codex can prepare shot variants and
do independent work until the relevant choice is needed. No approval by silence.

**Exit:** every shot maps to the canonical tale or a labelled staging inference,
has a finite asset/action list, and can explain what the audience should see.
The first deliverable is a board, not a claim that animation exists.

## Phase 5 — voice, timing and bilingual polish

Begin voice selection/read-through preparation during Phase 1; complete final
recording and synchronization here before locking exports. Owner recordings are
the current default. A synthetic voice/provider route requires an explicit
choice, and imitation of a person's voice is not assumed.

**Codex deliverables:** approved EN/FA recordings and transcripts referenced in
the manifest; edited cue timings; music/ambience/effects with actual rights and
gain records; caption tracks and language routing. Prevent clipping, overlapping
unintended voices, stale audio after seek and captions obscuring hands/props.
Default is narration with existing bounded expression states; no fake mouth
flapping or claimed phoneme lip-sync. Lip-sync is a separate optional scope.

**Exit:** human listening/read-through review in both languages, exact cue/text
parity with the approved adaptation, natural pacing and an agreed final duration
for each edition. Do not perform final visual lock before this timing review.

## Phase 6 — final renders and independent quality checks

**Codex deliverables:** full EN and FA rendered files, clean opening/ending,
caption files, screenshots of difficult shots and a reproducible export command.
Use the selected format and installed toolchain; verify actual container/codecs,
resolution, frame rate, frame count, duration, audio streams and ending with
`ffprobe`. Do not invent an MP4 because Godot produced an AVI or image sequence.

Check normal playback, direct seek, scene boundaries, reset/replay, missing input
failure and language switching. Inspect complete exported movies, not just the
Godot preview: beard/alpha edges, near/far occlusion, repeated props, exact fire
count, caption cuts, Persian joins/direction and audio sync. Report technical
and human performance review separately.

**Exit:** both editions have actual complete reviewed renders and no known
blocker affecting story, rendering, text, timing or audio. Caption-only/silent
delivery may be separately approved, but must not be labelled voiced completion.

## How to continue with Codex

Use this work loop for each bounded unit:

1. Read the current board, actual files, status and scoped approvals.
2. Choose the next unblocked unit and state its output and exit checks.
3. Implement in the candidate; preserve original art and unrelated outputs.
4. Run focused tests, render the affected shots, inspect the actual results and
   fix failures before extending scope.
5. Save evidence and update [production status](chapter-01-animation-status.json).
   Distinguish implemented, technically checked and owner-approved.
   After **each bounded part**, create an optional external-review prompt naming that
   part's actual files, commands, evidence, limits and questions. Preserve the
   returned review separately; Codex reproduces findings and records repairs.
   Qwen review does not grant owner art, casting or voice approval. Do not send
   data to a provider automatically; the owner can run the prepared prompt.
6. Continue automatically into routine implementation. Request an owner choice
   only when it changes art, casting, recording or accepted content, and present
   a concrete result/options. Never stop merely to ask whether to run a test.

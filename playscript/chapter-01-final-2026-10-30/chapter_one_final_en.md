# Ancient Tablet
EDITORIAL FINAL FOR OWNER REVIEW; NOT A FINAL PICTURE, AUDIO OR TIMING APPROVAL
Original teaching fiction, not history or reconstructed Shahnameh.

## S01 — Something in the Mud
Northern riverbank | Near sunset; rain

[Rain strikes the dark water. Chapar’s horse shies beside the road. Chapar dismounts. Something is caught among the mud and reeds.]

[Chapar lifts a clay tablet from the mud with both hands. Its edge is broken and its seal impression damaged. He gently clears the writing, then rests a finger beside the broken seal.]

On-screen text: Before sunrise, count the watchfires beyond the water.

**Chapar:** A royal seal... or what is left of one.

[His eyes move from the tablet to the far bank. Distant lights flicker through the rain; their purpose cannot be made out from here. Chapar wraps the tablet and secures it inside his saddlebag.]

**Chapar:** I only found you. That is what I must tell them.

[He mounts and rides towards the city. The tablet stays secured in the saddlebag, never in his riding hand.]

## S02 — The Road to Court
City approach and palace entrance | Dusk deepening into night

[The city emerges through the rain: high walls, lit roofs and stone palace columns. This is a fictional world, not a reconstruction of a historical period.]

[Behind Chapar, the gate stands open to ordinary traffic. He dismounts at the stable and takes the wrapped tablet from the saddlebag. Rainwater runs beside him in a narrow channel.]

[He pauses before entering the hall, tightens the cloth around the tablet’s broken edge, then crosses the threshold carrying the same bundle.]

## S03 — The King’s Question
Royal hall | Early night

[Tahmures sits on his throne. Beside him, Shahrasp closes a document. Wet and breathless, Chapar offers the bundle with both hands.]

**Chapar:** My king, I found this beside the river. I did not see who carried it.

**Tahmures:** Then that is your news: a tablet found, its bearer unknown. Come closer.

[Tahmures takes the bundle and opens it on the low table beside the throne. He sees the broken seal, but his eyes move to the writing before his hand reaches it.]

**Tahmures:** “Before sunrise, count the watchfires beyond the water.”

**Shahrasp:** “Beyond the water”... which water?

**Chapar:** I found it by the northern river. I know no more than that.

**Tahmures:** You did well to bring it. Shahrasp, take it to the Divan House. Ask them: “What is this?”

**Shahrasp:** What should they establish, my king? Who owned the tablet, or what its writing means?

**Tahmures:** First let us hear what they say.

[Shahrasp hesitates, then takes the tablet in its cloth. Chapar stays near the door. Tahmures withdraws his hand from the empty table.]

## S04 — Three Readings
Divan House | That night

[Akvan Div sits beside several open records. Black Div faces Akvan; Div-e Sefid sits nearest the door. Shahrasp places the tablet at the centre of the round table.]

**Shahrasp:** Greetings. The king has sent this tablet and asks: “What is this?”

**Akvan Div:** Where was it brought from?

**Shahrasp:** Chapar found it beside the northern river. We know neither its bearer nor its intended recipient. Report what you find to the hall.

[Shahrasp leaves. Akvan turns the tablet towards the lamp and compares the letter shapes with a record.]

**Akvan Div:** I have seen this turn of the hand... where?

[Black Div waits until Akvan sets the tablet down, then picks it up and reads under his breath.]

**Black Div:** “Before sunrise, count the watchfires beyond the water.” There is little time before morning.

[He sets the tablet on the table, rolls up his sleeves and leaves. Akvan is still searching the records for similar writing.]

**Div-e Sefid:** If this is for the border, Arash should see it. What use is it sitting here?

**Akvan Div:** We do not yet know whom it is for.

**Div-e Sefid:** And if the time is gone before we find out?

[Div-e Sefid wraps the tablet in the same cloth and takes it away. Akvan rests a hand where it lay, then picks up the old record. Three tasks have begun; nobody has confirmed which task was requested.]

## S05 — A Message Without an Addressee
Sepid Fortress, the fictional Iran–Turan frontier | Night

[After dismounting, Div-e Sefid hands the bundle to Arash. Arash opens it on the watch desk. The window looks towards the river.]

**Div-e Sefid:** It came from the king’s hall. I thought it should reach you without delay.

**Arash:** The king sent it?

[Div-e Sefid looks at the tablet. Before he answers, Arash reads the inscription and traces the broken seal with a finger.]

**Arash:** Send a patrol beyond the water. Close the gate until the riders return. Spread no word of war; we have seen no enemy.

[Riders cross the courtyard. Arash wraps the tablet again and hands it back to Div-e Sefid.]

**Arash:** Return this to the king. Tell him I have sent riders to inspect those fires. I need a clear answer: what are we to expect?

[Div-e Sefid secures the tablet in his saddlebag. As he passes the gate, he hears the wooden bar slide into place.]

## S06 — Before the News
Market below Sepid Fortress | Night

[The riders leave by the narrow road beside the market. A vendor pulls a basket clear of the horses. Hoofbeats overlap with the closing gate.]

**Market voices:** Have they shut the gate?

**Market voices:** Did you not see the riders? There must be a war.

**Market voices:** War? My goods are still outside!

[One shutter comes down. The next vendor puts out a lamp. A hand sets a half-filled basket on the ground; footsteps hurry out of frame. No enemy army is shown.]

[A brief view across the river shows charcoal burners tending their kilns. Light comes from kiln mouths, not watchtowers. The audience sees this; the court does not yet know.]

## S07 — The Cost of Three Answers
Royal hall | Late night

[Akvan opens an old record. A brief mental image of Hushang is superimposed on the letter shapes, then fades. He is not present in the hall; the image is not proof that he wrote the tablet.]

**Akvan Div:** The tablet’s hand resembles Hushang’s writing. I see a likeness; I do not yet know whether it is his.

[Tahmures is about to speak when a drop strikes the stone. Black Div stands in the doorway, wet to the waist. He wrings out a sleeve.]

**Black Div:** There were eleven fires.

**Tahmures:** You crossed the river?

**Black Div:** The tablet said to count them.

**Tahmures:** I asked what the tablet was.

**Black Div:** The tablet asked for a count.

**Shahrasp:** Were they watchfires?

**Black Div:** Charcoal kilns. Eleven of them.

[Div-e Sefid enters and sets the tablet on the table with both hands, still in its travelling cloth. Tahmures looks at the tablet, then at the water around Black Div’s feet.]

**Div-e Sefid:** Arash has sent riders and closed the gate. People in the market are saying there is a war. He asks what he should expect.

**Tahmures:** Did I say to send riders?

**Div-e Sefid:** No. I thought the message was meant for him.

[Tahmures rises, but holds back the sharp order on his tongue. Shahrasp opens the tablet’s wrapping.]

**Shahrasp:** All three have worked. They were not doing the same work.

**Tahmures:** And I did not say which work I wanted.

**Tahmures:** The fires were kilns; we have no report of an enemy. Tell Arash not to send anyone into a confrontation while we establish what happened. I will send a full reply.

[Div-e Sefid nods and leaves to carry the message; the tablet stays on the table. Akvan and Black Div withdraw at the king’s quiet signal.]

**Tahmures:** Shahrasp, stay. Bring the feather.

## S08 — The Unfinished Question
Royal hall, beside a brazier | The same night

[Shahrasp lays the feather beside the brazier. Tahmures picks it up and drops it into the fire. Flame rises; when it settles, Simurgh stands beside the brazier in bird form. The wings fold. No human body or transformation is shown.]

**Simurgh:** What has happened, that you burn the feather at this hour?

**Tahmures:** I sent a tablet to be understood. One crossed the river; another set riders on the road. The market is shut, and we still do not know whose tablet it is.

**Simurgh:** What did you ask them to do?

**Tahmures:** I asked: “What is this?” I should have been clearer: weigh every word and tell me exactly what it means.

**Simurgh:** “Means” is still carrying the weight of every question.

[Tahmures reaches for the tablet. Simurgh’s gaze directs his hand instead towards the blank sheet beside it. Shahrasp draws four spaces on the sheet.]

**Simurgh:** Who said it? To whom? For what occasion and purpose? And what do you want the divs to do now?

[Shahrasp writes: “Speaker”, “Recipient”, “Occasion”, “Requested result”. Under the first three he places question marks. His pen waits above the fourth.]

**Tahmures:** Tell us what kind of object it is, and list separately what we do not know. Not carry out its command.

**Simurgh:** Then that is your request. Write it on your own sheet, not in the gaps of the past.

[Shahrasp fills the fourth space: “Classify the object; state what is known and unknown; do not execute the tablet’s text.” The three question marks remain.]

## S09 — One Sentence, Three Functions
Hall table; three separate illustrative inserts | Continuing that night; illustrative time unspecified

[In close-up, Shahrasp writes on a separate sheet: “Close the western gate.” Simurgh looks at it. The original tablet remains at the edge of the table in the framing shot.]

**Simurgh:** If Arash says this to his guard?

[Illustrative insert: Arash points towards a gate. An unnamed guard begins drawing one gate leaf inward. Cut before the gate closes completely. This is not a new event in the main story.]

**Tahmures:** It is an order to carry out.

**Simurgh:** If the same sentence is read out in a court?

[Illustrative insert: a hand sets a written record before a judge. A gate appears only as a drawing on the record; no actual gate closes.]

**Shahrasp:** It is evidence of what was said, not a fresh order to a guard.

**Simurgh:** And an apprentice copying it to practise the letters?

[Illustrative insert: a pen copies the sentence again. The ink of the new line is still wet. Return to the hall table.]

**Tahmures:** It is writing practice. No gate should close because of it.

**Simurgh:** The words are the same. What they are doing here is not.

[Shahrasp puts the example aside, keeping it separate from the real message for Arash. Tahmures draws a fresh sheet towards him.]

## S10 — From Tale to Engineering
Separate educational graphics, outside the ancient story | Educational interlude

[A clear cut to an interlude: the hall gives way to modern educational graphics. The narrator addresses the audience; ancient characters do not speak this terminology.]

**Narrator:** The tablet’s sentence did not change; the role assigned to it did. A prompt is likewise more than the user’s last sentence: it is the structured interaction an application assembles for a model.

On-screen text: Prompt = instructions + current task + context + data + examples + constraints + output contract + interaction state

**Narrator:** An instruction gives behavioural direction; the current task states the work to do now. Context changes how that work should be interpreted; data is the material to examine or transform. Examples demonstrate desired behaviour. Constraints specify required and prohibited properties. The output contract defines the result’s form and meaning for its consumer; interaction state carries forward what earlier turns have established.

**Narrator:** Inference parameters and tool permissions affect the interaction too, but they belong to application configuration, not prompt prose. There are five useful review questions:

On-screen text: 1. Who assembled this element?
2. What authority should it have?
3. What role does it play?
4. Which consumer uses the result?
5. What observable result counts as success?

**Narrator:** These questions expose two mistakes: treating all visible text as equally authoritative, and attributing the whole application’s behaviour to the model. In the tale, the king’s ambiguous request and the tablet’s delivery path enabled the failure; it cannot be reduced to the divs misunderstanding words.

**Narrator:** The analogy has a boundary: the divs are fictional characters, not real models. Labels and clearer sentences do not create permission by themselves. Identity, execution authority and output checks must be enforced by application code.

On-screen text: Before judging the answer, record what each piece of language is doing here.

[Hold the final sentence. Shahrasp writes the same sentence on a separate sheet; then cut to that sheet in the hall. Persian and English text are prepared for separate language editions, never superimposed together.]

## S11 — The Message That Brings Them Home
Hall, then Sepid Fortress and market | Before dawn

[Tahmures sits across from Shahrasp, no longer speaking down from the throne. The ancient tablet lies in its cloth on the left of the table. A new message is written on a separate sheet to the right.]

**Tahmures:** Write: “Arash, the found tablet was not an order from me today. The fires are charcoal kilns. I did not make the task clear. Recall the riders and correct the rumour of war. Confirm receipt of this message and report the result.”

**Shahrasp:** I will send this under today’s seal. The tablet stays here.

[Div-e Sefid has returned. Shahrasp hands him the new message; he reads the addressee and closes the packet.]

**Div-e Sefid:** For Arash. I will bring back acknowledgement and the result.

[Cut to Sepid Fortress. Arash checks the new seal, reads the message and seals a short reply. At his signal, the gate opens.]

**Arash:** Message received. The riders are returning. Tell the market there was no confirmed report of war; people inferred it from the closing gate.

[The riders pass slowly through the market instead of galloping. A vendor lifts a shutter and picks up the abandoned basket. The customers have gone; a smile cannot undo the lost trade.]

**Narrator:** The reply came back: the message had arrived and the riders had returned. This time clarity did not depend on length or ornament. The message named its recipient and task, and asked for a result that could be checked.

[In the hall, Shahrasp places the sealed reply beside the new message. Tahmures looks at the three question marks on the first sheet. None has been erased.]

**Tahmures:** We still do not know who owned the tablet. Record that too.

# Tahmures Narrative Continuity

This is an editable source file. Update it in the same change as any chapter
that introduces or changes a recurring character, location, object, fictional
rule, relationship, or unresolved thread.

## Characters

### Tahmures

**Current understanding:** Language does not carry function, evidence,
authority, or permission by grammar alone. Reliable systems require governed
context, anchored evaluation, regression protection, source-owned trust
boundaries, least-privilege approvals, versioned operations, and mechanisms
capable of enforcing each hard requirement.

**Unresolved weaknesses:** Now teaches mechanism-first design, but governance
across kingdoms, institutions, and changing public values remains open.

**Important relationships:** Increasingly treats the First Scribe as an
experimental partner. Respects the Keeper of Scripts for structural questions,
the Archivist for provenance, and the Gatekeeper for refusing unsafe action.

### The First Scribe

**Role:** Records prompts, predictions, variants, outcomes, and corrections.

**Knowledge:** By Chapter 24, records template versions, contract violations,
workflow attempts, response distributions, human rubric evidence, context
selection, retrieval paths, memory lifecycle events, tool decisions, agent
state transitions, and hand-off acknowledgements rather than preserving only
successes.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1–12. Introduces the experiment ledger in
Chapter 1; adds source columns in Chapter 2; records requirements in Chapter 3;
reconstructs the archive bundle in Chapter 4; draws authority precedence in
Chapter 5; creates the first stratified example experiment in Chapter 6.
In Part II the Scribe becomes an experimental partner: tests invalid template
values, formalises workflow boards, records repeated trials and human rubric
scores, and measures context by evidence position.
In Part III the ledger becomes a system trace: selection reasons in Chapter
13, retrieval and citation evidence in Chapter 14, supersession and deletion
events in Chapter 15, tool receipts in Chapter 16, goal-version events in
Chapter 17, and correlated hand-off records in Chapter 18. In Part IV it
becomes a release and operations record: rubric reviews, protected regressions,
trust findings, approval digests, segmented telemetry, and residual risk.

### Keeper of Scripts

**Role:** Examines syntax, representation, labels, and structured expression.

**Knowledge:** Grammar suggests but does not establish role. Formatting can
communicate a boundary but cannot enforce authority.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1, 4, 7, 8, and 18.

### The Archivist

**Role:** Preserves context, provenance, and the reasons behind past decisions.

**Knowledge:** Records may remain valuable after their instructions expire;
selection and authority must be represented separately.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 2, 4, 6, 8, 12–15, 19, and 21. In Chapter 13, the
Archivist learns that exhaustive preservation does not create a useful working
set. In Chapter 14, she separates candidate retrieval from supported answers.
In Chapter 15, she helps distinguish durable memory from authoritative state.

### The Gatekeeper

**Role:** Controls consequential action and challenges language-only safety.

**Knowledge:** Clear words and convincing seals do not replace authenticated
authority. Same-level conflicts require escalation.

**Previous appearances:** Introduced indirectly in Chapter 4, centrally in
Chapter 5, as the owner of the divided key ring in Chapter 16, and as the owner
of trust partitions and exact approvals in Chapters 21–22.

### The Messenger

**Role:** Carries context and exposes what changes across recipients.

**Knowledge:** In Chapter 1, treats an unidentified record as an urgent current
message, contributing to the frontier alarm.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1 and 18. The Chapter 18 relay reveals how
individually reasonable summaries can lose constraints and negation.

### Mirror Div

**Role:** Produces exceptionally coherent completion without guaranteeing
support.

**Knowledge:** Can distinguish written, inferred, and unknown claims when the
interaction requires it.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 2.

### Literal Div

**Role:** Follows surface wording while exposing missing role and authority
structure.

**Knowledge:** Responds correctly to clearer separation in Chapter 4; is not
portrayed as malicious.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1, 4, and 13.

### Wandering Div

**Role:** Exposes goal drift and hidden assumptions during long work.

**Knowledge:** A list of completed verbs does not prove that intermediate
claims were validated or the mission succeeded.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 9 and 17. In Chapter 17, useful local action
continues after the goal changes, showing that persistence is not authority.

### Many-Voiced Council

**Role:** Makes response variance and legitimate alternatives visible.

**Knowledge:** Repeated voices are candidates, not votes; application policy
owns constraint checks and selection.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 10.

## Recurring Locations

### Hall of Scripts

**Meaning:** Language can be represented and classified in many forms without
its current function being self-evident.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 1.

**Rules established:** An object's wording, material, route, and interaction
role are distinct.

### Royal Archive

**Meaning:** Preserved context, provenance, memory, and the danger of granting
old words current authority.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 2, 4, and 6.

**Rules established:** Historical instructions remain evidence; records are not
destroyed merely because their authority expires.

### Council Chamber

**Meaning:** Stakeholder requirements, trade-offs, evaluation, and accountable
human judgement.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 2, 3, and 6.

### Border Gate

**Meaning:** Validation, authentication, permission, escalation, and the
separation of recommendation from action.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 4, 5, and 12.

### Workshop of Mechanical Scribes

**Meaning:** Repeatable production, interface compatibility, and the limits of
grammatical uniformity.

The mechanical scribes make repeatability visible without implying judgement.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 7 and 8.

**Rules established:** Mechanical scribes render and format quickly but do not
validate domain meaning or receive authority merely by completing a form.

### Western Mountain Pass

**Meaning:** Multi-stage work, changing external state, and the visibility of
assumptions at hand-offs.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 9.

### Upper Garden

**Meaning:** Creative exploration constrained by physical reality, public use,
and accountable human judgement.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 11.

### Chamber of Answers

**Meaning:** The difference between a complete archive and a task-specific,
auditable working set.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 13.

**Rules established:** Each temporary context table records inclusions,
exclusions, conflicts, scope, validity, and remaining budget.

### Map Room

**Meaning:** Retrieval can locate plausible text without establishing current
support.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 14.

### Northern Gate

**Meaning:** Read and write capabilities must be separated by mechanisms, even
when one physical key or interface once combined them.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 16.

### Provincial Relay Towers

**Meaning:** Meaning, authority, and state can decay at every hand-off even when
each component behaves plausibly in isolation.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 17 and 18.

## Recurring Objects

### The experiment ledger

**Meaning:** Prompts and examples become engineering artefacts when predictions,
cases, variants, outcomes, and regressions are recorded.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1–6.

**Rules established:** Preserve failures, sources, held-out cases, and evaluator
definitions—not only preferred outputs.

### Seal-cords

**Meaning:** Claimed authority, provenance, and the need for authentication.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 1, 4, and 5.

**Rules established:** Shape and position do not prove authority; custody and
current scope matter.

### The bronze balance

**Meaning:** Explicit evaluation criteria and stakeholder trade-offs.

**Previous appearances:** Introduced in Chapter 3.

**Rules established:** Its weights record human values; they are not revelations
from a div.

### Empty seals and the variable register

**Meaning:** Reuse turns language into an input-bearing program; openings need
names, types, defaults, and validation.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 7.

**Rules established:** Rendering and dispatch are recorded as separate events.

### The four validation gates

**Meaning:** Parsing, structural validity, semantic validity, and source
support are related but independent checks.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 8.

### The red checkpoint cord

**Meaning:** A visible boundary where evidence is inspected before
high-consequence continuation.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 9.

### The stitched decree

**Meaning:** Context can preserve every record in storage while omitting the
decisive item from a bounded interaction.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 12.

### The context selection report

**Meaning:** A context package is accountable for excluded and conflicting
records as well as included ones.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 13.

### The evidence ledger

**Meaning:** A correct answer is not accepted unless its retrieved source IDs,
freshness, scope, and support can be inspected.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 14.

### The ivory memory ledger

**Meaning:** Remembering is a governed write with subject, scope, provenance,
expiry, correction, and deletion—not passive transcript accumulation.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 15.

### The divided key ring

**Meaning:** Inspection and mutation require distinct capabilities, policy
checks, approvals, and receipts.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 16.

### The recall bell and run record

**Meaning:** Goal versions, budgets, cancellation, and terminal states belong
to the controller rather than generated language.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 17.

### The hand-off envelope

**Meaning:** Constraints, authority, artefact provenance, unresolved questions,
and permitted next actions survive transfer as validated fields.

**Previous appearances:** Chapter 18.

### The anchored court docket and protected regression ledger

**Meaning:** Evaluation records criteria, anchors, disqualifiers, reviewer
evidence, adjudication, old obligations, and release trade-offs.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 19–20.

### The Whispering Tablet and expiring storehouse seals

**Meaning:** Relevant content does not gain instruction authority; legitimate
authority is still bounded by exact principal, action, arguments, scope, and
expiry.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 21–22.

### The segmented operations map and workshop control table

**Meaning:** Rare failures remain visible and attributable, while language,
schema, code, storage, access control, and human judgement own distinct duties.

**Previous appearances:** Chapters 23–24.

## Fictional-world Rules

1. Divs can read many scripts and produce capable language, but their answers
   do not carry inherent authority or guaranteed truth.
2. Div behaviour depends on wording, context, examples, and surrounding
   arrangements; tendencies are not fixed moral identities.
3. Seals signify claimed authority but can be obsolete, out of scope, damaged,
   or forged.
4. Archives preserve causes as well as current rules, so old imperative text
   cannot simply be destroyed.
5. Tahmures cannot solve an engineering failure through unexplained magic or
   arbitrary command.

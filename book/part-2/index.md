# Part II — Prompt Design and Controlled Generation

Part II moves from analysing individual prompts to designing bounded
interactions. Tahmures and the First Scribe treat templates as application
code, make outputs machine-checkable, decompose work into observable stages,
and separate response generation from selection. They then test creative work
against human criteria and confront the practical limits of long context.

The learning arc is deliberately cumulative:

1. validate reusable inputs before rendering;
2. validate outputs before consuming them;
3. validate intermediate states before advancing a workflow;
4. make selection policy explicit when several answers are defensible;
5. separate creative exploration from accountable acceptance; and
6. select context by purpose, priority, and provenance rather than length alone.

Each chapter includes a committed offline experiment and matching Python and Go
implementation. The recorded fixtures are synthetic observations for learning,
not evidence that one prompt will dominate across providers or tasks.
